2:30–3:10. First finding, and it is not obvious. The price field in the June file is a quote for one stay near the scrape date, mostly the week of 15 June, with later quotes in match weeks. Compare the same 2,865 listings against the September file and June costs 16 percent more, and up to 37 percent more in match weeks. Taking the price at face value would have lifted our core yield from 2.3% to 3.0%. Decision one: we divide June quotes by 1.14. Winter prices run 12 percent below September, so we also build a price index by month rather than using one flat price.
